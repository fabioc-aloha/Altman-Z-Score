Applying turnaround theory, Starbucks is in a pre-recovery phase. According to Hofer (1980), the company must first stabilize cash flows and reduce leverage before pursuing growth initiatives. The marginal Z-Score improvement suggests that operational restructuring efforts are beginning to take effect, but liquidity and balance sheet weaknesses still dominate.

In line with Bibeault (1999), immediate priorities should focus on liquidity preservation—negotiating with creditors, reducing short-term debt, and improving working capital management. Mid-term, strategic initiatives around cost control and asset efficiency are essential. Long-term growth depends on restoring profitability and equity strength, which requires disciplined capital allocation and operational excellence.

Given the distress level, the renewal process should emphasize stakeholder communication, transparency regarding liquidity challenges, and targeted operational improvements. The company's trajectory aligns with a cautious turnaround—urgent stabilization is needed before aggressive expansion.
