### 4. Turnaround & Renewal Theory Application

Given Duke Energy’s **Distress Zone** status, turnaround theory emphasizes urgent retrenchment and financial stabilization:

- **Hofer (1980)** suggests immediate retrenchment actions such as cost cutting, asset sales, and cash preservation to halt decline.
- **Bibeault (1999)** highlights the importance of diagnosing root causes (likely leverage and profitability issues here) and stabilizing operations.
- **Hoskisson et al. (2004)** recommend strategic restructuring focusing on core regulated utility operations to restore financial health.
- **Freeman (1984)** stresses stakeholder alignment, critical here to maintain investor, creditor, and regulator confidence during turnaround.
- **Beard (2024)**, while focused on tech, underscores innovation in business models; for Duke, this translates to investing cautiously in renewables and efficiency to future-proof operations without overextending financially.

Urgent creditor negotiations, refinancing, and transparent communication are essential. Strategic investments should be limited to those with clear, near-term cash flow benefits.
