### 4. Turnaround & Renewal Theory Application

Given AAL’s **Distress Zone** status, turnaround theory prescribes urgent retrenchment and cash preservation strategies. According to Hofer (1980), the initial turnaround phase should focus on stabilizing liquidity and halting financial decline. Bibeault (1999) emphasizes the importance of retrenchment—cutting costs, divesting non-core assets, and renegotiating debt terms.

Hoskisson et al. (2004) recommend strategic restructuring to realign operations with cash flow realities, while Beard (2024) highlights the need for innovation even in distressed firms to reposition competitively post-recovery. Freeman’s (1984) stakeholder alignment theory suggests transparent communication with creditors, employees, and shareholders to maintain trust during crisis.

For AAL, immediate focus should be on:

- Aggressive cost control and operational efficiency improvements  
- Negotiating with creditors to restructure debt and avoid covenant breaches  
- Preserving cash and managing working capital tightly  
- Preparing for potential asset sales or capital raises if feasible  
- Engaging stakeholders with clear, honest communication to maintain support
