Definitions & Glossary

Altman Z-Score

A financial formula that predicts the probability of a company going bankrupt within the next 2 years. Developed by Edward I. Altman in 1968.

Z1 (Original Model)

The original Altman Z-Score model developed for public manufacturing companies. Uses the following cutoffs: Safe Zone: > 2.99, Gray Zone: 1.81-2.99, Distress Zone: < 1.81.

Z2 (Private Firm Model)

Altman Z-Score adaptation for private manufacturing companies. Uses the following cutoffs: Safe Zone: > 2.9, Gray Zone: 1.23-2.9, Distress Zone: < 1.23.

Z3 (Service Company Model)

Altman Z-Score adaptation for non-manufacturing companies and emerging markets. Uses the following cutoffs: Safe Zone: > 2.6, Gray Zone: 1.1-2.6, Distress Zone: < 1.1.

Working Capital / Total Assets

Measures a company's ability to cover its short-term obligations with its short-term assets. Indicates liquidity and operational efficiency.

Retained Earnings / Total Assets

Measures cumulative profitability over time and the company's age. Indicates how much a company has reinvested its earnings into itself.

EBIT / Total Assets

Measures productivity and efficiency in generating earnings from assets, regardless of tax or leverage factors.

Market Value of Equity / Book Value of Total Liabilities

Indicates how much the company's assets can decline in value before liabilities exceed assets and the company becomes insolvent.

Sales / Total Assets

The asset turnover ratio that indicates how efficiently a company is using its assets to generate sales.

Book Value of Equity / Total Liabilities

Used in the Z2 model to replace market value with book value for private companies. Measures long-term solvency.

Safe Zone

Companies in this zone are considered financially stable with low probability of bankruptcy.

Gray Zone

Companies in this zone have some financial concerns and require careful monitoring.

Distress Zone

Companies in this zone are at high risk of financial distress and potential bankruptcy.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

A momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements. Values above 70 indicate overbought conditions; values below 30 indicate oversold conditions.

MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

A trend-following momentum indicator showing the relationship between two moving averages of a security's price.

Bollinger Bands

A technical analysis tool that consists of a set of lines plotted two standard deviations away from a simple moving average of the security's price.

P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings Ratio)

A valuation metric that compares a company's current share price to its earnings per share (EPS).

Beta

A measure of a stock's volatility in relation to the overall market. A beta greater than 1 indicates above-average volatility.

Sharpe Ratio

Measures risk-adjusted return. Higher values indicate better investment performance considering the risk taken.

Max Drawdown

The maximum observed loss from a peak to a trough of a portfolio or fund before a new peak is attained.

Volatility Risk

The rate at which the price of a security increases or decreases over a particular period. Higher volatility means higher risk.

Market Cap

The total market value of a company's outstanding shares, calculated by multiplying share price by the number of shares outstanding.
