### 4. Turnaround & Renewal Theory Application

Given Realty Income’s **Distress Zone** status, turnaround theory emphasizes urgent retrenchment and cash preservation (Hofer, 1980; Bibeault, 1999). The company should prioritize:

- **Retrenchment:** Immediate cost-cutting, divestiture of non-core or underperforming assets to improve liquidity.
- **Cash Preservation:** Tight working capital management and delaying non-essential capital expenditures.
- **Creditor Negotiations:** Proactively engage lenders to restructure debt, extend maturities, or reduce interest burdens.
- **Stakeholder Alignment:** Following Freeman (1984), align creditors, shareholders, and management around a realistic recovery plan to maintain support.

Strategic restructuring (Hoskisson et al., 2004) should focus on stabilizing core property income streams and exploring innovative leasing or property management models (Beard, 2024). Given the real estate sector’s cyclical nature, renewal efforts must balance short-term survival with positioning for eventual market recovery.
