### 4. Turnaround & Renewal Theory Application

Given JPM’s **Distress Zone** status, turnaround theory recommends urgent retrenchment and financial stabilization. According to Hofer (1980), the sequencing should begin with crisis management focusing on cash preservation and creditor negotiations. Bibeault (1999) emphasizes diagnosing root causes of failure, which for JPM may include excessive leverage or operational inefficiencies.

Hoskisson et al. (2004) suggest strategic restructuring, potentially involving asset divestitures or capital injections. Beard (2024) highlights the importance of innovation even in distress, but only after stabilizing core operations.

Freeman’s (1984) stakeholder alignment theory underscores the need to engage creditors, shareholders, employees, and customers transparently to rebuild trust.

Recent market optimism may complicate turnaround efforts, requiring clear communication to align perceptions with reality.
